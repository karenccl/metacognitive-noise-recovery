# Metacognitive noise recovery with `hmetad`

**Static meta-d′ estimation under dynamic confidence generation and distributional misspecification.**
A reproducible R/Stan simulation study (no human data). Draft report: [manuscript/report.md](manuscript/report.md).

## Question

- **RQ1 (G1).** If confidence is generated by a drift-diffusion process with post-decisional readout noise σ_c, does posterior `log_M` from a static Gaussian hierarchical meta-d′ model (`hmetad`) fall as σ_c rises?
- **RQ2 (G2).** If the signal distribution is Gumbel-min, what does fitting the default Gaussian model cost, compared with the correct family?
- **RQ3.** How often, and where, do fits fail diagnostics?

Simulation is needed because real data have no known noise parameter. The study cannot show that human confidence works this way, and it does not claim `log_M` numerically recovers σ_c (different scales); only ordering is tested.

## Design

| Generator | What it is | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| G0 | Data from the equal-variance Gaussian meta-d′ model, known `log_M = -1` | 240 |
| G1 | DDM + unbounded post-decision accumulation + readout noise σ_c (low/medium/high, calibrated) | 720 |
| G2 | Gumbel-min signal distribution, known `log_M = -1`, each dataset fitted under the correct and the Gaussian family | 480 |

Grid per cell: N ∈ {20, 40} participants × T ∈ {100, 200, 400} trials × d′ ∈ {0.75, 1.5}, 20 replications, K = 4 confidence levels. Fitted model: hierarchical `hmetad`, `normal(0,1)` prior on population `log_M`, 4 chains × (1000 + 1000), one escalation retry if flagged. See `docs/design_decisions.md` (locked decisions, dated deviations) and `docs/analysis_log.md` (what was run, in order, including bugs and corrections).

## Headline results (all from `data/processed/`)

- **G1 ordinal recovery:** level means of `log_M` ordered low > medium > high in 12/12 cells (adjacent differences 0.46–1.20, smallest 7.2 Monte Carlo SEs); ordering low > medium > high held in 100% of matched replications in 11 cells and 95% in one; 720/720 fits passed. (Fig. 3)
- **G0 calibration:** pooled 95% credible-interval coverage 95.8% (95% CI 92.5–97.7%, MCSE 1.3%); small negative `log_M` bias in every cell (−0.18 to −0.03), source not established. (Fig. 2)
- **G2:** LOO preferred the correct family in 96.2% of 240 paired datasets; Gaussian misspecification biased `log_M` upward in all 12 cells (+0.06 to +0.20). 8–12% of LOO observations have Pareto k > 0.7 (no remedy applied). (Fig. 4)
- **Model adequacy:** posterior predictive discrepancy was small for all five representative fits, including the misspecified one, so small PPC error did not flag the misspecification. (Fig. 5; one replicate per fit)
- **Failures:** 1,437 pass, 3 fail, 0 error out of 1,440; failures confined to d′ = 0.75 cells.

Results are conditional on the simulated processes. Limitations are listed in the report (Section 5); the main ones are that RQ1 is a comparatively favourable test, the small negative bias is only partly characterised, the K = 6 (G1 only) / prior / PPC checks are small, and there is no JAGS cross-check or real-data illustration.

## Installation

Requires R 4.5.0 and a C++ toolchain for `rstan`. `hmetad` 0.2.1 comes from the metacoglab r-universe, `rstan`/`StanHeaders` 2.36.0.9000 from the stan-dev r-universe.

```r
renv::restore()
```

**Sampler:** all results were produced with `brms`'s default **`rstan`** backend. Leave `brms.backend` unset (or `"rstan"`); setting it to `"cmdstanr"` would use a different sampler and will not reproduce the published draws bit-for-bit. CmdStan is not needed. (An earlier version of this README, and of the environment notes, wrongly said CmdStan was used; see `docs/computational_environment.md`.)

## Reproduction

What can be regenerated from the tracked files alone: the figures (`make figures`), since they read only `data/processed/*.csv` and `config/`.

What needs the fits: `compile_results.R`, `compute_primary_metrics.R`, `loo_comparison.R` and `ppc_joint_counts.R` read `data/interim/`, which is git-ignored. Regenerate it with the grid run (resumable; safe to re-invoke):

```bash
make test        # unit tests, fast
make all         # setup, grid, G1, G0, G2, compile, analyse, figures
make robustness  # prior sweep (~20 min) and K=6 refits (~75 min)
```

The full grid took about 26 hours on one 22-core Windows machine (5 concurrent fits × 4 chains, about 55 fits/hour, thermally throttled). Lower `execution.max_concurrent_fits` in `config/design.yml` on a smaller machine. Only replications 1–2 of each cell keep the full `brmsfit`; every fit keeps a compact record (posterior draws for population-level effects and SDs, aggregated counts, diagnostics).

## Diagnostic and failure policy

A fit passes if max R-hat < 1.01, bulk and tail ESS ≥ 400, and there are no divergences or maximum-treedepth hits. A flagged fit is refitted once with `adapt_delta = 0.99` and treedepth 14; if still flagged it is labelled `fail`. Pre-registered analysis rule: the primary analysis includes `pass` and `fail` fits (failures flagged), the sensitivity analysis is pass-only, and `error` rows would count as convergence failures. Diagnostics and elapsed time describe each row's final attempt only.
