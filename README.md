# Metacognitive noise recovery with `hmetad`

**Static meta-d′ estimation under dynamic confidence generation and distributional misspecification.**
A reproducible R/Stan simulation study, extended in v3 (2.0.0) with three further simulation analyses and an empirical analysis of the Confidence Database. Draft report: [manuscript/report.md](manuscript/report.md) (draft v2.0, including the v3 results: §§3.6 and 4.8–4.11).

Of the pre-submission analyses in `docs/v3b_plan.md`, work packages B and C (spurious group differences when groups differ in d′; report §§3.7, 4.12) were completed on 2026-10-15 under tag `v3b-prereg`; the rest, including the unequal-variance check D, remain deferred (`docs/deferred.md`). v3 adds: the misspecification shift relative to the correct model (step 1), exact-refit LOO robustness (step 2), a difficulty-mixture simulation G3 (step 3), and the empirical comparison of Gaussian vs Gumbel-min on real data (step 4). See the v3 section below, `docs/v3_plan.md`, and `docs/design_decisions.md` (v3 section, including all deviations).

**Status of v1.2 (2026-09-29):** all 1,440 fits, analyses, figures, report and supplement are complete and pushed; the current release is tag `v1.2.1` (v1.2.0 plus engineering fixes). The report is a draft and has not been peer reviewed; references were checked against Crossref on 2026-09-28. See [docs/deliverables_checklist.md](docs/deliverables_checklist.md). The main finding is RQ2: Gaussian misspecification is visible to LOO and biases `log_M`, and aggregate PPC discrepancy also carries the signal when compared with the correct model, although it looks small in absolute terms. v1.1 reverses the v1.0 claim that aggregate PPC did not reveal the misspecification (report §4.4); v1.2 adds a practical single-model check with replicated data. RQ1 is a validity check.

## Question

- **RQ1 (G1; validity check).** If confidence is generated by a drift-diffusion process with post-decisional readout noise σ_c, does posterior `log_M` from a static Gaussian hierarchical meta-d′ model (`hmetad`) fall as σ_c rises?
- **RQ2 (G2; main finding).** If the signal distribution is Gumbel-min, what does fitting the default Gaussian model cost, compared with the correct family?
- **RQ3.** How often, and where, do fits fail diagnostics?

Simulation is needed because real data have no known noise parameter. The study cannot show that human confidence works this way, and it does not claim `log_M` numerically recovers σ_c (different scales); only ordering is tested.

## Design

| Generator | What it is | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| G0 | Data from the equal-variance Gaussian meta-d′ model, known `log_M = -1` | 240 |
| G1 | DDM + unbounded post-decision accumulation + readout noise σ_c (low/medium/high, calibrated) | 720 |
| G2 | Gumbel-min signal distribution, known `log_M = -1`, each dataset fitted under the correct and the Gaussian family | 480 |

Grid per cell: N ∈ {20, 40} participants × T ∈ {100, 200, 400} trials × d′ ∈ {0.75, 1.5}, 20 replications, K = 4 confidence levels. Fitted model: hierarchical `hmetad`, `normal(0,1)` prior on population `log_M`, 4 chains × (1000 + 1000), one escalation retry if flagged. See `docs/design_decisions.md` (locked decisions, dated deviations) and `docs/analysis_log.md` (what was run, in order, including bugs and corrections).

## Headline results (all from `data/processed/`)

- **G1 ordinal recovery:** level means of `log_M` ordered low > medium > high in 12/12 cells (adjacent differences 0.46–1.20, smallest 7.2 Monte Carlo SEs); ordering low > medium > high held in 100% of matched replications in 11 cells and 95% in one; 720/720 fits passed. (Fig. 3)
- **G0 calibration:** pooled 95% credible-interval coverage 95.8% (95% CI 92.5–97.7%, MCSE 1.3%); small negative `log_M` bias in every cell (−0.18 to −0.03; mean −0.075) that shrinks with data and correlates with the posterior between-participant SD, but whose cause was not tested. (Fig. 2)
- **G2:** LOO preferred the correct family in 96.2% of 240 paired datasets; Gaussian misspecification biased `log_M` upward in all 12 cells (+0.06 to +0.20); 95% interval coverage of the true `log_M` was 97.1% (correct) vs 81.2% (Gaussian). 8–12% of LOO observations have Pareto k > 0.7 (no remedy applied). (Fig. 4)
- **Model adequacy (revised in v1.1):** across all 240 G2 datasets the Gaussian fit's aggregate PPC discrepancy was small in absolute terms (median RMSE 0.009 vs 0.003 for the correct family) but larger than the correct fit's in 96.7% of datasets, concentrated at the highest confidence level. Calibrated against the correct family's discrepancies (an oracle reference an analyst would not have), it detected the misspecification in 87.5% of datasets, at least as often as LOO's > 2 SE criterion in every cell. The two are not like-for-like (oracle reference versus a single-dataset decision), so this does not mean PPC is more sensitive than LOO. The v1.0 reading of one replicate ("small, so not flagged") was wrong. (Figs. 5 and 6; `data/processed/v2/`)
- **Practical PPC check (v1.2):** using only each fitted model's own replicated data (24 datasets with stored full fits), a discrepancy on the highest-confidence cells flagged the Gaussian fit in 20/24 datasets with 0/24 false alarms. The aggregate RMSE flagged 16/24 (the same as LOO > 2 SE on those datasets; oracle 22/24) and only 2/8 at T = 100. The participant-level X² also flagged 9/24 correct fits, so it is not usable. Small sample: two datasets per cell. (Fig. 7)
- **Failures:** 1,437 pass, 3 fail, 0 error out of 1,440; failures confined to d′ = 0.75 cells.

Results are conditional on the simulated processes. Full limitations are in the report (Section 5). The main ones:

- RQ1 is a comparatively favourable test of the static model.
- The small negative `log_M` bias is only partly characterised.
- The K = 6 (G1 only) and prior-width (n = 1 per generator) checks are small; G0 sensitivity to bin count is untested.
- The all-G2 PPC analysis uses posterior-mean predictions and an oracle reference; the replicated-data check that removes the oracle covers only 24 datasets (two per cell). G0/G1 have one PPC replicate each. Why the participant-level X² flags correct fits is untested.
- Several priors (flat d′/criterion intercepts, `student_t(3, 0, 2.5)` SDs) are unvaried brms defaults.
- LOO Pareto-k values are unreliable for a substantial share of observations.
- One seed base, one environment, rstan only; no JAGS cross-check or real-data illustration.

## v3 results (all from `data/processed/v3/`; figures in `outputs/figures/v3/`)

Simulation (no new human data):

- **Misspecification shift, same dataset (step 1, Fig. 6).** log M(Gaussian) − log M(correct) is +0.19 to +0.25 at d′ = 0.75 and +0.11 to +0.15 at d′ = 1.5; larger at low d′ in 6/6 cells (pooled +0.097, SE 0.011). It does not shrink with more trials (slope on log₂T ≈ 0), while the Gaussian intervals narrow, so the share of Gaussian intervals missing the truth rises with T (0.13 → 0.35 at d′ = 0.75). The correct fit's small negative bias matches G0's.
- **LOO robustness (step 2, Fig. 7).** On the 24 stored G2 pairs, 142 exact leave-one-participant-out refits left every PSIS conclusion unchanged (sign and > 2 SE agreement 24/24; median |Δelpd| 0.03).
- **Difficulty mixture G3 (step 3, Fig. 8).** Pooling trials of two difficulties (d′ 0.5 and 2.0) did not make LOO prefer Gumbel-min: 0/20 replications under both mixture and control; the true Gaussian model won by > 2 SE in 20/20 everywhere. Pre-specified verdict: no pooling artefact. On Gaussian data the misspecified Gumbel-min fit sits ~0.46 below the true log M, so a large between-family log M difference is not by itself evidence for either family.

- **Spurious group differences (v3b, 2026-10-15; `outputs/figures/v3b/fig_10_dprime_confound.png`).** For two groups that differ only in d′ (true log M equal), the Gaussian fit's 95% interval for the log M difference excluded zero in 3.3% of pairs with separate fits (correct model 2.5%; highest cell 0.15 at N = 40, T = 400) and in 2/20 with one model and a group effect (correct 1/20). Pre-specified framing rule (tag `v3b-prereg`): not met, so the shift is reported as mainly common to both groups, affecting absolute log M more than group comparisons at these sample sizes. Results: `data/processed/v3b/`.

Empirical (Confidence Database, Rahnev et al., 2020; Fig. 9):

- 45 usable datasets (17 primary perception, 7 staircase, 4 unrecorded-difficulty, 17 memory). Rules, coding and selection were fixed and tagged (`v3-prereg-empirical`) before any real-data fit. Grouped 5-fold cross-validation by participant.
- **Most datasets could not be categorised.** Under the pre-registered rule (both families must pass diagnostics), 15/17 primary datasets are unreliable. The 2 categorised primary datasets favour the Gaussian model. In memory, 5 favour Gumbel-min, all from one lab and paradigm (old/new recognition, 3-level confidence), 1 favours Gaussian.
- **The failures are one-sided.** The Gaussian fit failed diagnostics in 76% of datasets vs 31% for Gumbel-min (21 vs 1 datasets where only one family failed), so the primary rule's exclusions are not random. Admitting fits that fail only on divergences (pre-specified sensitivity): primary 3 Gaussian, 2 inconclusive; memory 6 Gaussian, 5 Gumbel-min, 2 inconclusive; no dataset changes direction.
- **Post-registration deviation.** After 16/18 initial fits failed diagnostics, all participant-level SDs got a `normal(0, 0.5)` prior and chains were lengthened (decided on convergence diagnostics only; validated in simulation; the original fits are kept as a comparison set). Details and all other deviations: `docs/design_decisions.md`.

v3 limitations: the empirical primary result rests on 2 (or 5 under the sensitivity rule) perception datasets; the memory result on one lab; Gaussian divergences on real data are a diagnostic signal, not evidence for Gumbel-min; the SD-prior check under heterogeneity tested one true SD (0.5) with 5 replications; LOO repair was not run for the 23 datasets that are unreliable under both rules; refit convergence was checked on one pilot dataset only.

## Installation

Requires R 4.5.0 and a C++ toolchain for `rstan`. `hmetad` 0.2.1 comes from the metacoglab r-universe, `rstan`/`StanHeaders` 2.36.0.9000 from the stan-dev r-universe.

```r
options(repos = c(CRAN = "https://cloud.r-project.org",
                  metacoglab = "https://metacoglab.r-universe.dev",
                  standev = "https://stan-dev.r-universe.dev"))
renv::restore()
```

The lockfile records these three repositories, so a plain `renv::restore()` should also find them; setting `repos` first is a safeguard. `Rscript code/00_setup/00_packages.R` then checks that everything needed is installed.

**Sampler:** all results were produced with `brms`'s default **`rstan`** backend. Since v1.2.1, `fit_one_hmetad()` passes `backend = "rstan"` explicitly, so a global `options(brms.backend = "cmdstanr")` no longer changes the sampler; a refit of grid cell 721 with the pin reproduced the stored draws exactly. CmdStan is not needed. (An earlier version of this README, and of the environment notes, wrongly said CmdStan was used; see `docs/computational_environment.md`.)

## Reproduction

What can be regenerated from the tracked files alone: the figures (`make figures`), since they read only `data/processed/*.csv` and `config/`.

What needs the fits: `compile_results.R`, `compute_primary_metrics.R`, `loo_comparison.R`, `ppc_joint_counts.R` and `g2_ppc_all.R` read `data/interim/`, which is git-ignored. Regenerate it with the grid run (resumable; safe to re-invoke):

```bash
make test        # unit tests, fast
# no make (e.g. Windows)? everything downstream of the fits, in order:
#   Rscript code/run_downstream.R
make all         # setup, grid, G1, G0, G2, compile, analyse, figures
make robustness  # prior sweep (~20 min) and K=6 refits (~75 min)
```

The full grid took about 26 hours on one 22-core Windows machine (5 concurrent fits × 4 chains, about 55 fits/hour, thermally throttled). Lower `execution.max_concurrent_fits` in `config/design.yml` on a smaller machine. Only replications 1–2 of each cell keep the full `brmsfit`; every fit keeps a compact record (posterior draws for population-level effects and SDs, aggregated counts, diagnostics).

### v3

Everything that needs no refitting (summaries, Figs. 6–9, tests) re-runs with `Rscript code/run_v3.R`, given the v3 fit files in `data/interim/v3_*`. The Makefile targets `v3-relbias`, `v3-reloo`, `v3-g3`, `v3-empirical-screen`, `v3-empirical-fit`, `v3-empirical-summary` and `v3-figures` regenerate everything from scratch. The empirical stage needs the Confidence Database in `data/raw/confidence_db/` (download and checksums: `docs/data_access_confidence_db.md`; raw data are not redistributed here). Long steps are resumable and shardable (`<shard> <n_shards>` arguments); measured times: G3 ~6.5 h, empirical fits ~55 h, grouped K-fold LOO repair (tiers 1–2) ~2 days on the same machine. Run `05b_loo_tiers.R` before `05_loo_repair.R`. Keep the machine awake during long runs and exclude `data/interim/` from any file-sync service.

## Diagnostic and failure policy

A fit passes if max R-hat < 1.01, bulk and tail ESS ≥ 400, and there are no divergences or maximum-treedepth hits. A flagged fit is refitted once with `adapt_delta = 0.99` and treedepth 14; if still flagged it is labelled `fail`. Pre-specified analysis rule: the primary analysis includes `pass` and `fail` fits (failures flagged), the sensitivity analysis is pass-only, and `error` rows would count as convergence failures. Diagnostics and elapsed time describe each row's final attempt only.

The v3 empirical fits use the same diagnostic thresholds with stricter sampling (`adapt_delta` 0.99, escalation 0.999 / treedepth 14, `init_r` 0.5, 2000 + 2000 draws) and a different use of failures: a dataset is categorised only if both families pass (divergence-only failures admitted in the pre-specified sensitivity analysis); see `config/v3.yml`.
